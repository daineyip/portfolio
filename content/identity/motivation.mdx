# Built by Humans

A few years ago, when I was building WorkInProgress from scratch, getting software
out the door was an exercise in constraint. Every feature was a negotiation with time
and resources. Today, those constraints have completely evaporated. Tools like Cursor,
Claude Code, and the agents we interact with daily make the cost of writing software
practically zero.

It's a rapid shift. Companies are all-in on AI, and hundreds of billions are being
spent to not fall behind. I have been thinking a lot about this shift, because when
building becomes frictionless it becomes terrifyingly simple to waste our global
resources building things that no one wants.

We are entering a time where engineering velocity is no longer a bottleneck, and what
has emerged is rather an importance of good judgment.

This is why I am invested in product management today. To bring real value to real
people. To design human-centric software that is unquestionably the best canvas for
its users. In a world where you can spin up an entire product in a weekend, the only
differentiation is the deep desire to fundamentally understand the people you're
building for.

Product excellence is developed through iteration, genuine curiosity about the user,
and a rigorous approach to craft. It necessitates deep, human intentionality, and
cannot be synthesized within the vacuum of a frontier-model.

The next generation world-changing products will be *constructed* with AI, but they
will be <u>built</u> by humans. I aim to create products that make a real difference
for real people, ensuring that all things that I build are inherently, and undeniably,
human.
